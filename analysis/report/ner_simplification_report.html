NER-guided simplification: what the results show

An analysis of the two-stage pipeline that first extracts clinical entities from a report and then asks the model to simplify it while keeping every extracted entity. It covers 15 reports (discharge summaries, prostate pathology, brain MRI) and a same-model, same-report comparison against the plain zero-shot prompt on 14 of them.

Data: analysis/results/ (evaluations.json, content_retention.json) Side-by-side outputs: Simplification Reader

01Key findings

  • +NER guidance makes simplifications more complete. In 14 paired reports (same model, same report), the NER-guided version kept more of the report's clinical entities in all 14 (91% vs 66% on average) and more of its numbers (61% vs 37%). The gain is largest in radiology and pathology, where findings, sizes, grades and margins are enumerable.
  • −NER guidance also turns inferences into stated facts. The extraction step is asked to infer normal ranges and list warning signs; the simplification step must mention every listed item. NER-guided outputs therefore contained invented warning-sign lists, a creatinine range for a patient with no creatinine test, a "son" who is never mentioned, invented dates and severity labels. Zero-shot outputs never produced these.
  • ±Zero-shot is easier to read, but has its own failure mode. Zero-shot versions were simpler in 11 of 14 pairs (grade 9.9 vs 11.0; reading ease 59.8 vs 53.9). Their errors are vaguer: added reassurance ("not new or dangerous", "no new or urgent problems"), rounded measurements ("the size of a small grape"), softened recommendations, and one invented lymph-node result.
  • −The extraction schema narrows what the model reports. The pathology schema has no field for stage or lymph nodes. Zero-shot Qwen mentioned lymph-node status in all 5 pathology reports; NER-guided Qwen in only 2. Whatever the schema omits, the guided output tends to omit too.
  • −No output reached the 6th-grade target. Across 50 usable outputs the mean grade is 11.4 (lowest 8.1), roughly unchanged from the originals, though reading ease rose to 53. The rule "keep the medical term and explain it in brackets" lengthens sentences and works against the grade target.
  • −The first run's outputs were partly invalid, and the metrics did not notice. Before the fixes, 11 GPT-OSS "simplifications" were the model's internal reasoning and 8 Gemini/Qwen outputs were cut off, all recorded as successes. The reasoning traces scored better than real simplifications on SARI and grade level. After fixing the pipeline and re-running, 50 of 59 generated simplifications are usable.
  • +Core facts were reliable. Gleason score, ISUP group and margin status were correct in every usable pathology output; the deceased patient was consistently described in the third person; the normal MRI was never given an invented abnormality.
  • ±This is mainly a Qwen study. Gemini 3.1 Pro has zero free quota and Gemini 3.8 Flash hit its daily cap, so every usable "Gemini" output came from gemini-2.5-flash (4 outputs). Qwen has complete data; GPT-OSS has 13 of 15 NER-guided outputs; Qwen was easier to read and more complete than GPT-OSS on the same reports.

02What was run

Pipeline. Stage 1 sends the report with a domain-specific extraction prompt that returns JSON: diagnoses, anomalies with values and normal ranges, procedures, medications, follow-up and warning signs for discharge summaries; specimen, grading, invasion, margins and IHC markers for pathology; exam details, normal and abnormal findings, measurements and impression for radiology. Stage 2 inserts that JSON as a "YOU MUST RETAIN ALL OF THESE" block into the simplification prompt. If stage 1 fails to parse, stage 2 uses the plain zero-shot prompt, which is identical apart from the entity block and the retention rule. The zero-shot baseline uses that same prompt.

Data. 15 curated reports: 5 discharge summaries (Brazilian hospital data, 201–1,370 words), 5 prostatectomy pathology reports (TCGA-PRAD, 159–493 words), 5 brain MRI reports (MR-RATE, 113–329 words). The target reader is a patient or family member in India, at a 6th-grade reading level.

03Which outputs are usable

Every output was checked for four failure modes before any metric was read: reasoning leak (the text is the model's scratch reasoning), cut off (ends mid-sentence), incomplete (under a quarter of the source length), and prompt echo (the entity block copied back with labels such as "[MILD]" and "MUST explain" instead of prose). The pipeline bugs behind the first two were fixed and the affected Groq trials re-run (section 08).

04How to read the automatic metrics

There are no gold-standard simplified versions, so every quality metric compares the output with the original report:

  • SARI uses the source as its only reference. Its ADD and DELETE parts are then always zero, so the score is one third of the n-gram KEEP F1: a measure of copying, not of simplification.
  • ROUGE, BERTScore and SBERT measure closeness to the original. BERTScore (roberta-large, no rescaling) sits between 76 and 88 for every output and does not separate conditions.
  • Flesch-Kincaid grade depends on sentence length. The sources are telegraphic fragments, so rewriting them as full sentences raises the grade even when words get simpler. Reading ease (FRE) moves in the expected direction.
  • Jargon reduction counts clinical terms. The prompt requires keeping each term and explaining it, so compliant outputs score 0% or negative.

Two reference-free checks were added: numbers kept (share of the source's numbers, dates and measurements found in the output) and entities kept (share of the report's reference entity list found in the output; the list is the union of all successful extractions for that report, so every output is scored against the same list). Both use automatic matching and under-count paraphrases. In radiology, "numbers" also include sequence names such as T1 and T2.

What this means

  • Radiology benefits most. With the list, every finding, size and sequence stayed in; without it, Qwen summarised ("one is about the size of a small grape, another is smaller") and dropped hedges such as "underlying cavernoma cannot be excluded".
  • Pathology benefits, with one blind spot. The list kept margins, percentages and IHC markers, but its schema has no TNM or lymph-node field, and the guided outputs dropped node status that zero-shot Qwen included unprompted.
  • Discharge summaries gain little. Entity coverage is similar (85% vs 81%) and zero-shot kept more dates, because the schema groups events by type rather than time. This is also where the guided outputs invented the most (warning signs, instructions, a relationship, a date).
  • Readability costs about one grade level. Keeping more entities and explaining each one makes longer, denser sentences.

Pooled view. Across all models, the 29 usable outputs that used an entity list kept 86% of entities and 55% of numbers; the 21 without an entity list kept 69% and 39%. The direction matches the paired result, but the pooled groups mix models and reports.

07Clinical quality review

Every usable output was read against its source. This is an automated read-through by Claude, not a clinician's judgement; the clinician review pack is meant to confirm or correct it.

What went well

  • Grading and margins were right. Every pathology output gave the correct Gleason score, ISUP group and R0/R1 status. Qwen's PATH-05 explanation of the R1 margin, with its 19 mm extent, is close to what a clinician would say.
  • Death was handled correctly. All usable DS-04 outputs used third person and past tense, factually and respectfully.
  • Normal findings stayed normal. RAD-01 was explained structure by structure in every version, with the one physiological variant called harmless.
  • Medications and plans were preserved. Drug names came with a plain purpose, and schedules such as vancomycin 0.5 g after each dialysis until 01/09/2021 were kept.
  • NER-guided outputs were better organised (diagnosis, course, procedures with dates, medications, follow-up), and consistently spoke to the patient or family as instructed.

What went wrong

Inferences stated as facts (NER-guided) 9 of 14 Qwen NER outputs; also GPT-OSS

The extraction prompt asks for normal ranges "if inferable from medical knowledge", a warning-sign list and severity labels; stage 2 must mention every item.

"Please return to the hospital immediately if… his ostomy output changes significantly." (DS-01; not in the source)
"His kidneys were critically affected, indicated by abnormal creatinine levels, which are normally around 0.6 to 1.2 mg/dL." (DS-01; no creatinine value exists)

Also: warning-sign lists on DS-02 and DS-03 (Qwen) and DS-03 (GPT-OSS), "Your son" (DS-02), intubation and CPR given invented dates (DS-04, both models), "moderate" otitis and "confirms acute (new)" abscesses (severity fields restated as findings), "more urgently" added to a routine recommendation (RAD-02).

Reassurance and speculation (zero-shot) 6 of 14 Qwen zero-shot outputs

Without the list, Qwen smoothed rather than enumerated. It added comfort the report does not give and guessed at next steps.

"This is a chronic finding… not a new emergency… No new or urgent problems were found." (RAD-05; the report makes no comparison)

Also: "not new or dangerous" (RAD-04), "likely involving strong antibiotics" (RAD-03), malignancy work-up softened to "conditions that might affect the bone marrow" (RAD-02), and "has not spread to lymph nodes" on PATH-03, whose report gives no lymph-node result.

Events moved in time 3 outputs

The discharge schema has a "symptoms at admission" slot. Qwen filled it with the final half-hour before death ("Upon admission, the patient showed… bradycardia, QRS widening, and worsening shock", DS-04), and GPT-OSS wrote "When the patient was admitted, the main symptoms were heart failure and an abscess". Qwen NER also had a pleurostomy "closed on the same day" (DS-03), which the source does not say.

Misread clinical shorthand both strategies

"E. coli multi S" (multi-sensitive) became "grown in multiple places" or "resistant to many antibiotics"; "heart failure profile C" became "a check-up of the heart" in two outputs (zero-shot Qwen got it right); "meropenem vancomycin replacement of KCl 6%" became "the antibiotics were replaced with KCl" (GPT-OSS, twice); muciloid (psyllium fibre) was called a stomach-lining protector; "01/22" (January 2022) was read as a day of the month; "the body was released" became "released to the family".

Lay-anatomy slips about 6 outputs

Several pathology outputs called the prostate apex the "top edge" and the base the "bottom edge", which is the reverse. GPT-OSS called a 5% tertiary pattern 3 "the most aggressive pattern" (PATH-04) and said the tumour extended "19 mm into the margin" (it runs along it). Lay explanations are unchecked model knowledge.

Schema gaps become omissions pathology

The pathology schema has no TNM or lymph-node field, so "pN0 (0/12): none of the 12 lymph nodes had cancer", arguably the most reassuring line for a prostate cancer patient, was dropped by most NER-guided outputs, while zero-shot Qwen stated it every time.

09Recommendations

  1. Ground the extraction in the source. Drop "infer normal ranges" and the warning-sign list, or put them in a separate "general information" block that stage 2 may use but must not present as the patient's own findings. Keep severity labels out of the retention block. This targets the main cost of NER guidance while keeping its completeness gain.
  2. Fix the schemas: TNM and lymph nodes for pathology; an event timeline (event plus date) for discharge summaries.
  3. Resolve the readability conflict. Allow dropping a term when a common word exists, ask for short sentences, measure readability with bracketed glosses excluded, and retry when the grade is above target.
  4. Use the clinician review. The review pack pairs Qwen's NER-guided and zero-shot versions for every report, blinded, so the doctor's ratings give a direct human answer to the NER question.
  5. Check faithfulness automatically. A sentence-level "is this supported by the source?" check would catch invented warning signs and reassurance at scale.
  6. Decide on Gemini. Either enable billing for Gemini 3.x or drop those slots from the study.

10Limitations

  • Small samples: one run per condition, 14 paired reports for the main comparison, fewer for the other models. Run-to-run variance (visible on DS-01, where two identical Gemini runs differed by 44 points on numbers kept) is not measured.
  • The quality review is an AI read-through, not a clinical assessment.
  • Entity and number matching is automatic and under-counts paraphrases; the reference entity list is itself model-extracted, which favours outputs guided by similar lists.
  • Source reports are translations with idiosyncratic wording, which affects both models and readability scores.
